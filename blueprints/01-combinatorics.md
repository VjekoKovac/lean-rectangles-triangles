# Theorem 1.2. Rectangles and isosceles triangles

## Statement correspondence

The ambient lattice is `RTS.Point = Fin 2 → ℤ`. The four vertices in `rectangleCount` run independently through the finite set $A$. They satisfy $a+c=b+e$ and $(b-a)\cdot(e-a)=0$. The two ordered base points in `pinnedTriangleCount A k` satisfy $(2k-a-b)\cdot(b-a)=0$. The apex runs through $A$ in both `triangleCount` and `maxPinnedCount`. Repetitions are allowed, including $a=b$. The maximum is `Finset.sup` on the naturals. For a nonempty set the degeneracies give at least $|A|^2$ triangles, so the real divisions use positive denominators.

| Paper | Lean | Quantification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathfrak R(N)$ and its pinned version | `boxRatio`, `pinnedBoxRatio` | Nonempty subsets of the integer lattice with every coordinate in the closed real interval $[-N,N]$ |
| $\widetilde{\mathfrak R}(n)$ and its pinned version | `sizeRatio`, `pinnedSizeRatio` | All finite lattice sets of exactly $n$ elements, anywhere in the plane |
| $(\log x)^{c/\log\log\log x}$ | `logGrowth c x` | The exponent uses division by the third iterated natural logarithm |
| Theorem 1.2 | `CombinatorialGrowth` | One absolute $c>0$, a real radius threshold and a positive natural cardinality threshold. Each assertion holds at every scale beyond its threshold. |

The radius suprema are maxima because a bounded lattice box has finitely many subsets and, for $N\ge0$, contains the origin. For exact cardinality the ratios form a bounded nonempty family. Each ratio is at most $n^4$ since all rectangle counts are at most $n^4$ and denominators are positive. Existence of a set of each finite cardinality follows from the infinitude of the lattice. The two comparisons between the pinned and unpinned extremal constants are theorems in `RTS.CombinatorialProof`.

## How the source proves growth

1. **Prime field, Lemma 2.2.** Take a prime $p\equiv3\pmod4$ and the circle $B_p=\{(a,b):a^2+b^2=1\}$ in $\mathbb F_p^2$. The proof pairs the slope $t$ with $((1-t^2)/(1+t^2),2t/(1+t^2))$ and adds $(-1,0)$. The checked equivalence `unitCircleEquiv` and its application `card_unitCircle` give $|B_p|=p+1$. Mathlib supplies the quadratic nonresidue criterion `ZMod.mod_four_ne_three_of_sq_eq_neg_one`. For vectors $J(a,b)=(-b,a)$, parametrize base and height by $rw,sJw$, with $w\ne0$. For each starting point the paper counts $4(p-1)p$ rectangles and $3(p-1)p$ triangles. For the latter count the difference-of-squares parametrization gives $\sum_t\chi(1+t^2)=-1$. Orthogonal changes of coordinates make the pinned triangle counts independent of the apex. These are counts of parameter tuples and include cases with zero side parameters.
2. **Chinese remainder theorem, Lemma 2.3.** For a nonempty finite set of such primes let $q=\prod_{p\in P}p$. Directions must be nonzero modulo *each* prime dividing $q$. The Chinese remainder isomorphism preserves the circle, rotation, halves and each vertex equation componentwise. Taking the product of the local counts gives ratio $(4/3)^{|P|}$ for both modular comparisons.
3. **Uniform lattice lifting, Lemma 2.4.** Write $\eta_q=8q^{-2}\prod_{p\nmid q}(1-p^{-2})$, where the product ranges over primes. Count primitive directions in each allowed residue class, omit $(r,s)=(0,0)$, and use the sup norm. For positive $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ and $N\ge q$, the unpinned main term for a set $E$ of classes is $16\eta_q q^{-4}\alpha^2\beta\gamma |E|N^4\log N$, with error $O_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(|E|N^4/q^4)$. For pinned classes $F$ the main term is $4\eta_q q^{-2}\beta\gamma |F|N^2\log N$, with error $O_{\beta,\gamma}(|F|N^2/q^2)$. The third estimate is $|A_N(q)|=4|B_q|N^2/q^2+O(|B_q|N/q)$. The constants are uniform in the residue classes and modulus. The proof counts one residue class, uses Möbius inversion for primitivity and partial summation for the inverse-square weights, then sums over classes.
4. **Fixed modulus, Proposition 2.1.** The selected lattice set is $A_N(q)=\{(a,b)\in[-N,N]^2\cap\mathbb Z^2:a^2+b^2\equiv1\bmod q\}$. Use rectangle parameters $(1/4,1/4,1/4)$, triangle parameters $(1,2,4)$, and pinned parameters $(2,4)$. Nonzero primitive-direction representations have multiplicity two from a simultaneous sign change. The triangle upper bounds may overcount the parity condition for an integral apex. The zero configuration is handled separately. The lower bound $\eta_q\ge8/(\zeta(2)q^2)$ lets a sufficiently large absolute $C_0$ absorb the relative errors when $N\ge\exp(C_0q^2)$. This yields both lattice ratios at least $c_0(4/3)^{|P|}$ with absolute $c_0>0$.
5. **All radii and exact cardinalities.** The first $m\ge2$ primes congruent to 3 modulo 4 have product $q_m\le\exp(C_1m\log m)$ by the prime number theorem in arithmetic progressions. Take $m=\lfloor c_1\log\log N/\log\log\log N\rfloor$ with sufficiently small $c_1>0$. For an exact cardinality take maximal integer $N$ with $|A_N(q)|\le n$. Add points from $A_{N+1}(q)$ until the size is exactly $n$. Monotonicity applies to counts, so use the numerator at $N$ and the denominator at $N+1$; the ratio itself need not be monotone. Since $n\le(2N+3)^2$, eventually $N\ge\sqrt n/4$. The uniform estimates at both radii give a fixed-modulus ratio bound for every $n\ge\exp(C_2q^2)$. The same logarithmic choice of $m$ with $n$ in place of $N$ finishes.

## Lean proof route

- `RTS.FiniteCircle` constructs the rational circle parametrization. For the character sum it identifies the pairs $(u,t)$ with $u^2-t^2=1$ with the units of the field by $u-t$; the inverse unit is $u+t$. It combines this bijection with mathlib's `quadraticChar_card_sqrts`.
- `RTS.FieldCounts` changes orthonormal bases, solves the two circle equations for the rectangle sides, completes the square for the triangle height, and counts the roots with the preceding character sum. Theorems `rectangles_card`, `triangles_card`, and `pinnedTriangles_card` include all admissible primes. The proof of the pinned formula explicitly rearranges the finite sum by apex.
- `RTS.CRT` and `RTS.CRTMembership` transport the local sets through mathlib's `ZMod.prodEquivPi` and prove the product formulas and both exact modular ratios. Membership is expressed in the coordinates of the cyclic group, permitting reduction of integer tuples.
- `RTS.CircleLift` defines $A_N(q)$ and proves it is nested with unbounded cardinality. The points $(1+qj,0)$ witness unboundedness. Configuration counts are monotone and any nonempty admissible finite set gives a lower bound on the relevant supremum.
- The lattice proof only requires a lower count of rectangles and an upper count of pinned triangles. For the lower estimate one can keep only $0<\|w\|_\infty\le N/(8q)$; both coefficient intervals then contain enough representatives even after the forbidden pair is removed. For the upper estimate both intervals can be enlarged to radius $4N/\|w\|_\infty$ and unrestricted directions can dominate the primitive ones. These choices change absolute constants. The headline conclusion uses one unspecified positive constant. Reproducing the three full asymptotic formulas of Lemma 2.4 is a distinct, stronger task.
- `RTS.LiftingAssembly` combines the uniform scalar bounds with the modular ratio. `RTS.LiftingInterpolation` relates adjacent scales via a factor of 32 and fills all intervening cardinalities. `RTS.choosePrimeCount` proves the floor optimization for arbitrary positive prefactor and absolute scale constant. `RTS.PrimeProducts` supplies the finite prime sets with the required product-size bound.

At the threshold $N\geq\exp(1000q^2)$, `RTS.Lifting.rectTupleSet_lower` retains at least $|E|N^4\log N/(1024q^6)$ rectangular tuples, and `RTS.Lifting.pinTupleSet_upper_threshold` bounds the pinned tuples by $10000|F|N^2\log N/q^4$. Here $E$ is the modular rectangle set and $F$ the modular pinned set at the reduced apex. Each integer rectangle receives at most two retained tuples. Each ordered base at an apex is covered by a pinned tuple, except for the single all-equal triangle. The latter contributes one. Using $|A_N(q)|\le 9|B_q|N^2/q^2$, `RTS.fixed_modulus_bound` takes the absolute prefactor $1/(1024\cdot2\cdot9\cdot10001)$. The small numerical prefactor is absorbed by the logarithmic choice of $m$ in the introductory theorem.
